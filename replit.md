# CertifyMe project guidance

## Marketing purpose

The university credential walkthrough is marketing material, not product documentation or a description of the demo interface.

- Write for university decision-makers: leadership, registrars and credential teams, CIOs, academic governance, continuing education, careers and employer-engagement teams.
- Lead with institutional needs, clear credential value and a relevant conversation with CertifyMe. Learner benefits support that buyer story.
- Avoid process commentary such as “Earlier layers stay in view,” “Connections so far” and “Layers explored.” Capability summaries should explain value, not viewing history.
- Keep navigation labels short and useful; basic Play, Pause, Previous and Next controls do not need marketing language.
- This is a marketing website, not an audit report or disclosure document. Across the entire website, remove unnecessary disclaimers and labels from product interfaces, dashboards, screenshots, workflows, records, analytics, charts, mockups and other marketing visuals. Do not replace them with another label.
- Do not add “illustrative interface,” “sample dashboard,” “example view,” “values shown are examples,” “not live customer data,” “not measured institution results,” “not audited institution outcomes,” “not product-wide benchmarks,” “example records,” or similar qualifications. Let the visuals and surrounding marketing copy speak for themselves.
- Keep the website confident, enterprise-grade, product-led, credible and aspirational—not defensive, legalistic, audit-like or over-qualified. Do not invent customer evidence, integration support, certifications, metrics, universal interoperability or career outcomes.
- Treat ICP messaging checks as editorial persona reviews unless actual customer research has been performed; never describe them as customer interviews or measured conversion results.
