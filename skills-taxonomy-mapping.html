Why institutions need a skills layer

Your Learning Has a Language. So Does the Workforce.

One department calls an outcome “data analysis”; another uses “applied analytics”. A professional standard and an occupational framework may describe related capabilities differently again. Without reviewed relationships between those terms, leaders cannot easily compare skills across programmes or explain how learning relates to external requirements.

How the model works

How Skills Taxonomy Mapping Works

Skills taxonomy mapping relates learning outcomes, competencies and credential achievements to structured skills concepts. The aim is a consistent institutional language—not a list of keywords pasted onto badges. A skills taxonomy organises skills and relationships; a competency framework can also define proficiency, assessment and professional requirements.

Programme→Learning outcome→Achievement evidence→Represented skill→Credential & learner record
01

Map learning outcomes

Relate course, module and program outcomes to defined skills and competencies.

02

Describe the skills associated with degrees, certificates, badges and professional awards.

03

Relate institutional terminology to external skill concepts where that connection is appropriate.

04

Keep institutional context

Preserve your program language and requirements alongside structured skill relationships.

05

Reuse reviewed mappings

Apply consistent, institution-approved terminology across related credentials and records.

For institutional decision-makers

One Skills Language. Different Decisions.

Give leadership a clearer view of programme capabilities, and give technology and professional teams a traceable way to interpret them.

01 / University leadership

Put Programme Skills Into Strategic Context

  • See across programmes. Compare skills represented by assessed outcomes and credentials.
  • Review relevance. Investigate how institutional capabilities relate to occupation requirements.
  • Keep decisions accountable. Review the evidence and mapping assumptions before acting on a supposed gap.
02 / CIO & digital transformation teams

Treat Skills as Governed Institutional Data

  • Define data boundaries. Agree where skills relationships live and how they connect to source records.
  • Preserve provenance. Distinguish issuer claims, local terms and external taxonomy references.
  • Plan interoperability. Discuss structured credential and learner-record outputs alongside existing systems.

Data location, supported integrations, permissions and export formats must be confirmed for each implementation.

For chartered & certification bodies

Keep Professional Standards at the Centre of Certification

A chartered body defines the standard and assesses the professional—not an external taxonomy. Reviewed skills relationships can help explain what its certifications and continuing professional development (CPD) represent, while preserving the authority of its own framework and evidence.

Evidence & standards

Skills Should Be Connected to Evidence

A skill claim is more meaningful when it is related to an achievement issued by an institution. Keep a clear chain between issuer, achievement, evidence, skill and learner record.

W3C Verifiable Credentials describe digitally verifiable claims. Open Badges 3.0 supports achievement and skill claims in portable credentials.

Institutional governance

Your Institution Defines What Its Learning Represents

Skills mapping should support academic and professional governance—not replace it with automatic suggestions. Define who reviews each proposed relationship and keep local claims distinct from external references.

01

Institutional ownership

Academic and professional teams define the meaning of outcomes, standards and credentials.

02

Reviewed mappings

Subject experts should approve a relationship before it is used to describe an achievement.

03

Evidence & provenance

Distinguish an issuer's assessed claim from a suggested external taxonomy reference.

04

Change over time

Plan how relationships will be revised as curricula and frameworks change; confirm versioning support during implementation.

The record layer

Keep Skills Connected to the Achievements Behind Them

A Comprehensive Learner Record can bring achievements, credentials and other learning evidence together. Skills Taxonomy Mapping interprets what those achievements represent, while the record preserves their issuer and context.

The record and skills layer complement each other; a skills summary cannot replace a verified achievement or transcript.
