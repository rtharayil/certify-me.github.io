One record, broader institutional evidence

Represent More of What Your Institution Recognises

A Comprehensive Learner Record (CLR) is a structured digital record of achievements, credentials and related learning evidence. Universities use the model to connect degrees, courses, micro-credentials, skills and experiences across systems while preserving who issued each claim. The CLR complements the transcript; it does not replace the authoritative academic record.

Provenance & verification

A Learner Record Should Be More Than a Static Document

A verifiable record depends on who issued an achievement, what was claimed and how its status can be checked. The 1EdTech CLR Standard describes a format for interoperable learner records; W3C Verifiable Credentials provide a model for machine-verifiable claims.

Across organisations

Learning Doesn't Come From One Institution

A learner's record may connect achievements from a university, professional body, training provider and employer. Each organisation remains the source of its own claims; bringing achievements together does not transfer or guarantee their validity.

Standards & governance

Standards First. Institutionally Governed.

1EdTech's CLR 2.0 standard, Open Badges 3.0 and the W3C Verifiable Credentials model provide relevant technical foundations. An institution still decides what an achievement means, what evidence supports it and who can issue it. A standard reference is not proof that the illustrated record is certified or independently verified.
