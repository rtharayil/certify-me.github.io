The decision: Which skills are taught, which are requested, and where should the programme respond?

The university challenge

The evidence exists. The connection is often missing.

Universities know what their programmes teach and what their learners achieve. Labour-market information sits elsewhere. Without a reliable link between the two, it is hard to see how a credential relates to occupations or where employer requirements are changing.

One connected solution

Credentials → Skills → Workforce → Careers

Workforce Intelligence is the connection between institution-held learning evidence and separately sourced market context—not a replacement for either.

  3. 03 / Workforce

  4. 04 / Careers

    What could the university do next?

The institutional evidence and external labour-market source remain distinct. A real implementation requires agreed mappings, coverage, refresh frequency and data permissions.

Questions for a connected market source

  • Which employers request these occupation and skill combinations?
  • In which regions and industries do relevant roles appear?
  • Which requirements are recurring or emerging?
  • How recent and representative is the underlying vacancy data?

Opportunity context, not a job board

Use job requirements as evidence for university decisions.

A vacancy feed can make the connection concrete: a role describes an occupation, its location and the skills an employer requests. A university can compare those requirements with the skills represented in a programme and decide where to deepen employer relationships or review curriculum.

The learner's ability to apply is a separate workflow. This page is for the university teams making decisions about programmes, employability and partnerships.

Standards & data foundations

Keep the source of every claim clear.

Open Badges 3.0 can represent an issued achievement, CLR 2.0 can bring achievements together, and a governed skills taxonomy can provide consistent skill concepts. Workforce Intelligence consumes those relationships; labour-market information does not become part of the credential itself.

Before comparing supply and demand, agree which achievement evidence counts, how skills are mapped, what market source is used and when its data was last refreshed.
