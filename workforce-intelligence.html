From academic records to institutional intelligence

Make programme evidence useful for workforce decisions.

Workforce intelligence for universities connects institution-held programme and credential evidence with reviewed skills relationships, occupations and separately sourced employer requirements. It gives academic, careers and leadership teams a common basis for investigating programme relevance, curriculum priorities and possible pathways. Reliable comparisons depend on agreed mappings and the coverage of the market source.

Skills intelligence in higher education

Compare what a programme evidences with what employers request.

Skills intelligence relates institution-reviewed learning evidence to skills and, where external data permits, occupation and employer requirements. First establish what a credential represents; then compare it with a separate market source. A possible skills gap is a prompt for investigation, not a measured deficit without matching definitions, geography and time period.

Standards & data foundations

Keep the source of every claim clear.

Open Badges 3.0 can represent an issued achievement; CLR 2.0 can bring achievements together. A governed skills taxonomy adds consistent skill concepts. Workforce Intelligence uses those relationships; labour-market information does not become part of a credential.

For each implementation, agree which institutional evidence counts, how skills and occupations are mapped, and which external source is authorised.

Data methodology

Agree the evidence before comparing supply and demand.

Institutional supply and external demand have different sources and definitions. A trustworthy comparison documents both; the exact labour-market methodology depends on the authorised provider and implementation.

Institutional evidence
Use institution-defined programmes, assessed outcomes and issued credential criteria; retain issuer and achievement provenance.
Skills & occupations
Review skill-to-achievement and skill-to-occupation relationships against the selected taxonomy and local context.
Market source
Document the provider's employer and vacancy sources, licensing, deduplication and definitions before using its signals.
Coverage & freshness
Record geography, industries, refresh date, salary or work-mode coverage where available, and limits or gaps in the source.

Workforce intelligence, explained

Common questions from university teams

What is workforce intelligence for universities?

It is a way to relate institution-held programme, credential and skills evidence to occupations and separately sourced labour-market information. University leaders can use those relationships to investigate curriculum relevance, employer requirements and possible career pathways. Reliable findings require reviewed skill and occupation mappings, a defined geography and a market source whose coverage and refresh dates are known.

How does it help academic and careers teams?

They can compare assessed programme skills with requirements for relevant occupations, identify questions for curriculum review and explain possible pathways. An apparent gap should be checked against the quality and timing of both sources.

How do credentials connect to employer demand?

Issued achievements provide institutional evidence; reviewed skills mappings interpret that evidence. Occupation relationships and an authorised labour-market source provide the external comparison. Employer demand is not embedded in the credential itself.

What is skills intelligence in higher education?

It is a traceable view of the capabilities represented by assessed learning and credentials. Skills Taxonomy Mapping helps maintain consistent concepts before a university compares them with occupation or employer requirements.

How can a university compare programme skills with the market?

Define the programme and assessed outcomes, review its skill mappings, select relevant occupations and compare them with employer requirements from a licensed source. Document geography, coverage and refresh dates before drawing conclusions.
