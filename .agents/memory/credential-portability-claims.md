---
name: Credential portability claims
description: Accuracy boundaries for credential portability, cryptographic verification and recipient identity claims.
---

Do not describe Open Badges or W3C Verifiable Credentials as making verification independent of every platform or vendor. Standards-based proof can be checked outside a single presentation interface, but ongoing verification can still depend on issuer keys and applicable status or revocation resources.

**Why:** Absolute portability claims overstate what credential standards guarantee and create an avoidable factual inconsistency across comparison pages.

**How to apply:** In migration and competitor comparisons, describe open standards as reducing dependency on a single interface or format, and advise buyers to confirm issuer-key, status-resource, export, and continuity arrangements in writing.

Cryptographic verification of credential-subject data is not independent verification of the recipient's real-world identity.

**Why:** Supplied conceptual verification artwork uses “Verified Identity” language, but a signature establishes the issuer's signed association with a subject, not evidence of a separate identity-checking process.

**How to apply:** In editable marketing copy, describe the issuer-identified credential subject or recipient association. Only claim independent identity verification when an actual identity-checking process is established; keep conceptual artwork distinct from live verification evidence.
