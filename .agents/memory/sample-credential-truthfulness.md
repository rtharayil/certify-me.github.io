The homepage interactive credential should use one coherent fictional university narrative, not a real person's linked credential. Its issuer, recipient, certificate artwork, proof details and role/skill relationships must agree. Do not turn this narrative into a claim of an actual customer deployment, issued award, certified taxonomy mapping or employer endorsement. FERPA is programme context, not an occupational skill or learner certification. Visual caption policy follows replit.md, which supersedes the earlier sample-disclosure requirements.

The user wants a guided visual walkthrough, not a live verification page. The approved direction prioritizes the credential as **institutional infrastructure**: progressively connect achievement, skills, structured vocabulary, learner records and workforce relevance, rather than giving QR codes, OTP or file uploads their own major chapters. Keep one credential as the visual anchor; do not turn workforce context into a learner job board. Autoplay should loop until the visitor interacts, then hand over control; respect reduced motion. Social sharing should link to the tour rather than a real person's issued credential. Do not invent successful verification results or represent the tour as an actual enrolment or active learner record.

Describe the walkthrough as connected **layers**, not steps, and show meaningful navigation labels rather than numbers alone. Consolidate redundant introductory headings and follow replit.md for clean marketing visuals. Treat institutional value as the concluding outcome, not another numbered layer. Prioritize readable explanation and useful visual space over fitting many tiny cards; merge closely related concepts rather than shrinking their content.

Subsequent spacing-only refinements still felt cramped and too small; they asked for a full UX redesign, deeper verification explanation, merged related concepts, and an unnumbered outcome.

Keep the current layer, upcoming layer and automatic/paused state visible as the tour scrolls; a navigation highlight alone is insufficient.

**Why:** The user explicitly said they could not tell that different layers were being shown or that another layer would follow.

**How to apply:** Preserve a persistent progress indicator when changing walkthrough layout, scrolling or playback.
