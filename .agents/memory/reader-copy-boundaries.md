description: Keep editorial taxonomy, claim approvals and authoring process out of public institutional copy.
---

Customer case studies should read as implementation cases, not marketing material.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested “this should look like a case study not marketing material” for the association narrative, then requested the same treatment for the workforce learning case.

**How to apply:** Keep future revisions centred on the organisation’s challenge, delivery and supported changes. Express longer-term capabilities conditionally, without repeated audit disclaimers, sales pitches or invented testimonials.

The user requires every page to be consumable by the institutional ICP, without visible editorial metadata or AI-style authoring footprints. Parent assignments, SEO pillars, owner approvals and audit process descriptions belong in internal records, not visitor-facing prose.

**Why:** The user found a shared “Put this resource in an institutional context” block explaining a “reviewed parent” and displaying generic standards links. Earlier page scores and model reviews missed these explicit defects.

**How to apply:** Review the complete rendered text, including shared sections, navigation and footer copy. Use direct phrase checks alongside semantic review; a model score or a model's silence is not sufficient. Keep useful source dates and substantive product requirements, but express marketing copy through institutional problems, capabilities and value rather than instructions to analyse or evaluate them. Do not force unrelated standards links onto resource articles. Follow the marketing-visual rule in replit.md; do not reintroduce disclaimer captions or replacement labels.

Do not invent personal experience in FAQs or turn an unsupported anecdote into a customer testimonial. Do not rewrite genuine attributed quotations merely because their style is informal.


**Why:** Broad style reviews missed unsupported first-person anecdotes and also proposed changing actual attributed customer quotes.

**How to apply:** Check attribution and provenance before editing quotations. Replace unsourced anecdotes with factual guidance or clearly hypothetical workflows without invented people, statistics or outcomes.

Instructional/research-response format is a separate defect from AI authorship. The underlying information can be correct and valuable while its published format is wrong for CertifyMe marketing.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejects public marketing that reads like an AI research answer, consultant checklist, decision framework or internal instruction set, including buyer-journey mapping and commands to confirm procurement or track evaluated outcomes.

**How to apply:** Preserve useful facts and necessary evidence boundaries, but write a confident enterprise SaaS narrative with a relevant CertifyMe action. Check title/body purpose together. Retain deliberately procedural API guides, account-help instructions and actual application controls; imperative grammar alone does not establish accidental instruction publication.
