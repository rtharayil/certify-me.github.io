# 16. FAQ

This section is important for SEO, but keep answers genuinely useful.

### What is skills taxonomy mapping?

Skills taxonomy mapping is the process of connecting learning outcomes, competencies, credentials or other achievement data to a structured classification of skills. It creates a consistent way to represent the capabilities associated with learning and professional achievements.

### What is credential-to-skill mapping?

Credential-to-skill mapping connects a credential — such as a degree, certificate, badge or professional certification — with the skills or competencies it represents.

### Why do universities need skills taxonomy mapping?

Universities can use skills mapping to create stronger relationships between academic programs, learning outcomes, credentials and workforce-relevant skills. It can also provide a structured skills layer for learner records and credentialing systems.

### Can institutions use their own skills framework?

Yes. A practical institutional approach should allow an institution's own terminology and competency structures to remain meaningful while creating relationships with external frameworks where appropriate.

### Can skills be added to digital credentials?

Yes. Standards such as Open Badges 3.0 support achievement and skill claims within portable credentials, and Open Badges 3.0 aligns with the W3C Verifiable Credentials model. ([1EdTech Standards][5])

### What is the difference between a skills taxonomy and a skills framework?

A skills taxonomy generally provides a structured classification of skills and their relationships. A skills framework can be broader, potentially defining competencies, proficiency expectations, outcomes or other structures around skills.

### Can skills mapping connect to occupations?

Yes. Some established frameworks explicitly connect skills with occupations. ESCO, for example, has separate skills and occupations pillars and provides relationships between them. ([EUSurvey][3])

### Does skills mapping replace a university transcript?

No. Skills mapping should complement existing academic records and credentials rather than replace them.

### Can mapped skills become part of a Comprehensive Learner Record?

Yes. Mapped skills can form part of a broader learner record alongside credentials, achievements and other learning evidence.
