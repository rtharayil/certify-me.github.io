# CertifyMe — Four Levels of Digital Credentialing

You are working on the existing CertifyMe website.

Main website:
https://www.certifyme.online/

The objective is to create:

1. A **compact section on the main homepage**
2. A **new, detailed standalone page** explaining the four levels of digital credentialing

This should become a major positioning and educational asset for CertifyMe.

---

# IMPORTANT BUSINESS CONTEXT

CertifyMe is a B2B digital credential platform.

We sell to organizations such as:

* Universities
* Colleges
* University departments
* Enterprises
* L&D departments
* Certification bodies
* Professional associations
* Training providers
* E-learning platforms
* Government organizations
* Other organizations issuing credentials

The purpose of this framework is NOT to say that every organization needs the most sophisticated credentialing system.

Instead, explain that organizations have different credentialing requirements.

There are four broad levels:

### Level 1

Traditional PDF / paper credentials

### Level 2

Digital recognition / branded digital badges

### Level 3

Verifiable digital credentials based on standards, trust and independent verification

### Level 4

Connected credential + skills ecosystem with records, skills, pathways and workforce intelligence

---

# CORE POSITIONING

CertifyMe is intentionally focused on **Levels 3 and 4**.

CertifyMe is NOT necessarily the right product for:

### Level 1

Organizations that simply need PDFs or basic certificates.

### Level 2

Organizations whose primary requirement is branded digital recognition, badge sharing and engagement.

CertifyMe becomes relevant when an organization needs:

### Level 3

Trusted, standards-based, independently verifiable digital credentials.

And becomes even more valuable at:

### Level 4

A connected credential ecosystem involving comprehensive records, skills, taxonomies, pathways, job/skill mapping and workforce intelligence.

Do not make Level 1 or Level 2 sound "bad."

They solve legitimate problems.

The message is:

> **Different needs require different levels of credentialing.**

---

# THE FOUR LEVELS

Use the following conceptual framework throughout both pages.

---

## LEVEL 1 — TRADITIONAL CREDENTIALS

### PDF & Paper

**Recognition without digital credential infrastructure.**

Typical characteristics:

* PDF / paper certificates
* Manual verification
* Static records
* Limited portability
* Easy to copy or manipulate
* Limited machine readability
* Limited automation
* Little or no credential analytics

Typical use cases:

* One-off certificates
* Basic awards
* Internal recognition
* Simple completion certificates

### CertifyMe fit

**Not the right fit when all the organization needs is a PDF certificate.**

Do NOT insult this category.

The message should be:

> If your requirement is simply to generate and distribute certificates, a full digital credential platform may be unnecessary.

---

# LEVEL 2 — DIGITAL RECOGNITION

### Branded Digital Badges

**Recognition, branding, sharing and engagement.**

Typical characteristics:

* Digital badges
* Custom branding
* Credential designs
* Social sharing
* Recipient engagement
* Shareable achievements
* Digital recognition
* Basic credential management
* Individual achievement recognition

Typical use cases:

* Short courses
* Workshops
* Events
* Marketing programs
* Community recognition
* Basic training
* Course completion
* Organizations primarily focused on branded recognition

Important:

Do NOT say digital badges have no value.

Do NOT say they are fake.

Do NOT claim they are insecure unless technically verified.

Instead:

> Level 2 is appropriate when the primary goal is digital recognition, branding and sharing.

### CertifyMe fit

**May be more than you need if basic digital recognition is your only requirement.**

The objective is to create a natural upgrade question:

> "Do we only need recognition, or do we need the credential itself to be trusted, portable and independently verifiable?"

---

# LEVEL 3 — VERIFIABLE DIGITAL CREDENTIALS

### Standards + Trust + Verification

This is where CertifyMe starts.

Core characteristics:

* Open Badges 3.0
* W3C Verifiable Credentials
* Cryptographically signed credentials
* Tamper-evident credential data
* Independent verification
* Machine-readable credential data
* Portable credentials
* Structured credential information
* Credential lifecycle management
* Credential verification
* Analytics
* Enterprise integrations

Use the phrase:

> **When recognition isn't enough, credentials need to prove something.**

# PAGE SECTION 5

## Cryptographically Signed. Independently Verifiable.

Use these concepts:

### Institution-controlled signing

Credentials can be cryptographically signed by the issuing organization.

### Tamper-evident

Changes to signed credential data can be detected.

### Independent verification

Where supported by the credential format and implementation, verification does not need to depend on a viewer having an account.

### Portable

Credentials are designed around open standards rather than being only a proprietary database record.

### Machine-readable

Structured credential data can be processed by software.

Do not use absolute security claims.
