## 3. Approved commercial structure

There must be exactly three plans:

- Essentials
- Professional
- Enterprise

The six-layer model is an internal product-analysis framework. It is not a pricing hierarchy, and it must not be represented as six plans.

### Essentials — approved entitlements

- Exactly 1 administrator.
- Exactly 3 credential templates.
- Zero departments.
- No department-management functionality or department add-ons.
- Standard CertifyMe branding.
- No custom branding or white-label entitlement.
- Foundational credential creation, issuance, management, sharing, and verification remain available.
- Do not artificially remove foundational trust or credential-lifecycle functions simply to differentiate higher plans.

### Professional — approved entitlements

- Exactly 10 credential templates.
- 1 department included.
- Additional departments are available as paid recurring add-ons.
- Department-level access control must be fine-grained and enforced.
- Custom branding starts at Professional.
- Preserve the existing approved Professional administrator allowance and all other currently configured limits unless an explicit approved change exists.
- An additional department does not automatically grant institution-wide administrative access.
- Department managers must be restricted to their authorized department and resources.

### Enterprise — approved positioning

Position Enterprise for universities, colleges, large institutions, professional bodies, public-sector organizations, and other organizations that need institution-wide governance and a managed implementation.

The Enterprise offer must communicate, where supported and commercially approved:

- Institution-wide administration and governance.
- Centralized branding governance.
- Advanced permissions and auditability.
- Enterprise integrations and identity capabilities.
- Security review and procurement support.
- Implementation planning, migration, integration, testing, training, and go-live assistance.
- Contractually defined support, service levels, and escalation.
- Deployment, data residency, and contractual arrangements where available.
- Optional advanced products and ecosystem capabilities.

Do not invent Enterprise prices, capacity limits, implementation fees, hosting options, security certifications, response times, SLAs, or contractual promises. Use approved existing configuration and commercial material. If a value is unavailable, use the approved sales-contact approach rather than fabricating it.

### Other quotas and limits

Preserve existing approved/configured limits for:

- Annual credentials issued.
- Recipients or learners.
- Projects.
- Other usage limits.
- API usage and integrations, where applicable.
- Existing add-ons and usage-based charges.

Do not substitute previously discussed illustrative quotas for the current configured values.

Display credentials, recipients, templates, administrators, departments, and projects as distinct concepts. Never imply that these quantities are interchangeable.

Clearly explain the relevant billing period, included capacity, add-on availability, and what happens when a limit is reached—but only where the behavior is confirmed by the existing implementation or approved commercial rules.

### Training providers
