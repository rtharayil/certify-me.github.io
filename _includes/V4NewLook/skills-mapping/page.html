01 / Start with institutional meaning

What Is Skills Taxonomy Mapping?

Skills taxonomy mapping connects an institution’s learning outcomes, achievement evidence and competency language to structured skill concepts. It gives teams a consistent way to describe and compare capabilities while preserving the programme, assessment and professional context behind each claim.

The mapped relationship is an interpretation for institutional review—not a replacement for an outcome, assessment, transcript or professional standard.

A governed relationship Outcome + assessment evidence + expert interpretation → reviewed skill relationship

A taxonomy offers shared terminology. It does not decide what a learner has demonstrated or certify competence on its own.

02 / Why credentials need a skills layer

Why Credentials Need a Skills Layer

A credential can confirm an achievement, but its title alone may not explain the assessed capabilities within it. A reviewed skills layer can make those capabilities easier to discover and compare without reducing a programme to a bag of keywords.

When departments use different language for related outcomes, mapped relationships can help academic teams see where descriptions align—and where context makes them distinct.

  • Keep skill claims connected to assessed outcomes and their evidence.
  • Compare related capabilities across credentials without erasing local meaning.
  • Give external framework references a clear source and review status.
  • Support curriculum conversations; do not treat mapping as a measure of quality or job readiness.

04 / Institutional skills vs external taxonomies

An institution’s outcomes and competency framework carry local academic or professional meaning. External taxonomies can offer reference concepts for comparison, but a relationship between them must be assessed rather than assumed.

06 / Review & governance

Review & Governance

A useful mapping is inspectable: reviewers can trace the proposed relationship back to the outcome, achievement and evidence that informed it.

What should a reviewer be able to inspect?

The source outcome and achievement, evidence or assessment criteria, proposed skill wording, framework source, reviewer decision and any local context needed to interpret the relationship. Confirm what implementation records and exposes during discovery.

How should external references be treated?

As sourced reference concepts until an institution has evaluated and approved a particular relationship. Framework mention is not evidence of native integration, equivalence, or a completed crosswalk.

  1. Institution owns the claim

    Academic and professional teams define what an outcome, credential or standard represents.

  2. Evidence grounds the interpretation

    Connect a proposed skill relationship to assessed work and criteria—not a credential title alone.

  3. Experts review before use

    Subject experts decide whether a relationship is appropriate for institutional representation.

  4. Provenance stays distinct

    Keep issuer achievement claims separate from local interpretations and external framework references.
