How learning evidence connects to workforce demand

Programme → Learning Outcome → Achievement Evidence → Credential → Skill → Learner Record → Occupation → Workforce Demand.

The institution governs the first five relationships. ESCO, O*NET and custom competency frameworks offer reference concepts; reviewed crosswalks retain the local academic meaning. A learner record preserves provenance, while occupational requirements and employer demand require distinct market evidence.

CLR versus other institutional records

RecordRelationship to a CLR
Academic transcriptThe authoritative academic record; a CLR complements it with broader achievements.
Single credentialAn issued claim about an achievement; a CLR can connect multiple achievements with their provenance.
Digital badgeAn achievement credential; it can contribute to a broader learner record.
Student information recordThe institution's operational record of enrolment, grades and administration; a CLR is not a replacement for the SIS.

From credential issuance to institutional records

CertifyMe's credential lifecycle connects institutional award approval with issuance and ongoing record maintenance. Open Badges 3.0 structures achievement information, while the W3C Verifiable Credentials model defines issuer, holder and verifier relationships. Supported formats enable exchange with compatible receiving systems.

Verification checks proof, issuer authority and applicable status resources. Institution-reviewed skills relationships connect assessed outcomes to capabilities; a Comprehensive Learner Record retains achievement provenance. Workforce intelligence then relates those skills to occupations and separately sourced market information—not a guarantee of employment.

Institutional authority, connected capabilities
