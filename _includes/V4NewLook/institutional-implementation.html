How learning evidence connects to workforce demand

Programme → Learning Outcome → Achievement Evidence → Credential → Skill → Learner Record → Occupation → Workforce Demand.

The institution governs the first five relationships. ESCO, O*NET and custom competency frameworks offer reference concepts; reviewed crosswalks retain the local academic meaning. From credential issuance to institutional records

Start with an institution-governed credential lifecycle: define the achievement and approval rules, issue the appropriate format, and maintain correction, expiry and revocation processes. Open Badges 3.0 describes achievement credentials; the W3C Verifiable Credentials model explains issuer, holder and verifier roles. Format support does not guarantee compatibility with every receiving system.

Verification checks proof, issuer authority and applicable status resources. Institution-reviewed skills relationships connect assessed outcomes to capabilities; a Comprehensive Learner Record retains achievement provenance. Workforce intelligence then relates those skills to occupations and separately sourced market information—not a guarantee of employment.

Plan the implementation with the right teams

Registrars agree issuance, corrections and authoritative records. Academic teams define assessed outcomes and review competencies. Career services decide how learners present evidence and investigate opportunities. CIOs assess API workflows, integration scope, security and contractual data residency, access controls and continuity before rollout.

Higher education is the strongest vertical, while certification bodies, enterprise, government and training providers can apply the same infrastructure to their own governed achievements. Explore the university implementation hub, platform evaluation guides and anonymous customer evidence.
