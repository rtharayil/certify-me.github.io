02

The institutional challenge

The transcript is one of the university's most important records. Managing it should not depend on manual workflows.

Requests often touch email, document preparation, printing or scanning, postage, follow-ups and repeated verification. Each hand-off adds work for registrar and student-service teams—and leaves learners asking about status.

Students and alumni need records at consequential moments: graduate admissions, employment, licensing, immigration, international study and credential evaluation.

A transcript request is not an isolated administrative transaction. It is part of the learner's academic and professional journey.

  • Manual requests and email follow-ups
  • Staff intervention and document preparation
  • Printing, scanning and postage
  • Repeated verification and fraud-check enquiries
  • Status questions across fragmented systems

07

Trust starts with the issuer

A transcript is valuable because the institution stands behind it.

Paper and digital records can be misrepresented or changed. Verifiable issuance helps recipients assess provenance and integrity without treating any single control as proof of everything.

  1. Institution
  2. Academic record
  3. Digital transcript
  4. Security / signature
  5. Verifier

Identity association, recipient-controlled access and cryptographic signing address different risks. Together, the appropriate controls help recipients establish provenance and detect tampering.

Premium capability · recipient identity

Bind the transcript to the right person.

Premium ID-based tagging associates a transcript with a supported primary identifier. Institutions can use supported identity references, such as a passport reference, to strengthen the connection between the record and its intended recipient.

Identity association helps connect a person to a record; it does not independently prove every aspect of transcript authenticity. Institutions choose the identifiers and process appropriate to their policy.
