01 / Credential accessibility & sharing

Make every achievement accessible, recognisable and ready to share.

The credential experience begins with a simple question: can the learner access the achievement easily — and can the institution’s recognition travel with them?

CertifyMe supports credential creation, reusable templates, institutional branding, QR-based access, unique credential URLs, manual and bulk issuance, API-based issuance and sharing.

02 / Standards & interoperability

Build credentials on standards — not inside a proprietary silo.

A credential becomes significantly more valuable when other systems can understand it. Standards describe structured claims and their relationships, not just the appearance of a document.

They reduce dependency on a single presentation format; practical verification still relies on issuer keys and applicable status resources.

A

Achievement data model

Open Badges 3.0

1EdTech

Open Badges represents an achievement as structured information rather than treating the credential as only a visual document. An issuer makes an assertion about a recipient and an achievement, supported by criteria and, where provided, evidence and related metadata.

Why independent certification matters

CertifyMe's Open Badges 3.0 Issuer certification is listed in the 1EdTech registry. Independent certification demonstrates conformance to a defined standard rather than relying only on a vendor’s statement that its platform supports it.

03 / Verification & trust

Prove three things.
Not just one.

Verification is not a single yes-or-no question. Issuer authenticity, recipient identity and document integrity are distinct trust questions. A method that addresses one does not automatically prove the others.

Verification pathways

Different checks.
Different trust questions.

Select a pathway to understand what it is designed to establish.

Recipient identity association

Bind the credential to an identity reference.

An institution can associate a credential with an approved identity reference belonging to the recipient. During verification, the relevant identifier can be used to confirm the credential’s intended recipient.

Credential↓Primary ID reference •••• •• 42↓Intended recipient

Recipient authentication

Verify control of the registered contact.

Where enabled and configured, a verifier can request an OTP to a registered email or supported contact. The recipient enters it to demonstrate control of that channel.

Verifier starts check↓OTP to registered contact↓Recipient enters code↓Authentication result

Document integrity

Drop the PDF. Check the signature.

Download the credential PDF and validate its detached public-key signature. This checks document integrity; recipient identity uses a separate verification pathway.

Original credential→Cryptographic signature→Uploaded PDF→Signature validation

A match supports document integrity only. A PDF signature alone does not prove recipient identity.

Structured proof

Verify the credential’s structured proof.

Open Badges 3.0 and W3C Verifiable Credentials can carry structured claims and proof. Verification examines credential data rather than relying only on visual inspection of a PDF.

04 / Skill taxonomy alignment

Move from “what was completed?” to “what capability does it represent?”

CertifyMe can connect institution-reviewed achievements and learning outcomes with structured skills. Frameworks such as ESCO, O*NET and institutional skill frameworks provide a common language; they do not replace academic judgement.

The institution remains responsible for determining what its programme teaches and what an achievement represents. Taxonomies provide a common language for representing those capabilities.
