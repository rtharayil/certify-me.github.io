01 / The infrastructure

What Is Digital Credential Infrastructure?

Digital credential infrastructure turns an organisation-approved achievement into a credential that can be issued, maintained, checked and shared. It connects the source of the achievement to the credential lifecycle without asking organisations to replace the systems they already rely on.

That matters because a document alone is difficult to update, verify consistently or interpret across systems. Structured credentials carry defined claims and issuer provenance; governance keeps those claims tied to the organisation’s award policies.

What the infrastructure handles

Credential creation and issuance workflows, hosted credential access, standards-based representation, verification pathways, status management, and optional links from achievements to reviewed skills or learner records.

02 / The credential lifecycle

The Credential Lifecycle

Each step has a distinct role. The issuing organisation remains accountable for what the achievement means.

  1. 01

    Create

    Define the achievement, issuer, recipient claims and relevant criteria.

  2. 02

    Issue

    Release credentials through manual, bulk or scoped system workflows.

  3. 03

    Verify

    Check issuer provenance, credential integrity and status using the applicable method.

  4. 04

    Manage

    Maintain credential records and lifecycle state, including expiry or revocation where applicable.

  5. 05

    Share

    Give recipients a digital credential representation they can access and share.

Issuance options and workflow scope

Credential workflows may support individual awards, bulk issuance and API-based issuance. The appropriate route depends on the source system, approved fields, permissions, award rules and agreed integration scope; a named system is not a promise of a prebuilt connector.

05 / Standards & interoperability

Standards & Interoperability

Standards describe the credential’s data and claims—not merely how a certificate looks. They support interoperability, while verification still depends on the relevant trust and status resources.

Achievement specification

Open Badges 3.0

An achievement-focused specification for structured claims about an issuer, recipient and achievement, with criteria and supporting evidence where provided.

Certification and implementation

CertifyMe supports Open Badges 3.0 as a certified issuer. Certification is scoped to the issuer capability; it is not a claim that every downstream verifier, wallet or service is certified.

View CertifyMe’s 1EdTech certification listing ↗
Credential data model

W3C Verifiable Credentials

A W3C model for machine-readable claims and cryptographic proof. Open Badges 3.0 is an achievement specification that can use this model.

What interoperability does—and does not—mean

W3C is a standards model, not a vendor certification. Portability and ongoing checks depend on the credential format and on accessible issuer-key and status resources used by the verifier.

06 / Verification & trust

Verification & Trust

Credential integrity or issuer provenance does not independently establish that the person presenting a credential is its recipient.

01

Who issued it?

Issuer provenance is checked against the issuer information and applicable trust material.

02

Has it changed?

A signature or proof can support integrity checking of the credential data or signed document.

03

Who is presenting it?

Recipient identity is a separate question requiring an appropriate identity-association or authentication method.

Verification methods and boundaries

Credential ID or QR: directs a verifier to a credential record; lookup alone is not cryptographic integrity proof.

Identity reference or OTP: may associate a credential with an approved recipient reference or show control of a registered contact, depending on configuration.

Signed PDF or structured credential: can help check document integrity or issuer proof. A successful check does not by itself confirm the presenter’s real-world identity.

Continuity: ongoing verification can rely on accessible issuer keys and applicable status or revocation resources.

07 / The issuer owns the meaning

Credential Governance

Governance defines which system is authoritative, which achievements qualify, what data may be used, who approves issuance and who stewards the credential after award.

CertifyMe provides the credential infrastructure around those decisions. It does not replace the organisation’s award authority, policies or responsibility for its claims.

Governance questions to settle
  • Which source is authoritative for achievement and recipient details?
  • Which criteria, evidence and fields belong in the credential?
  • Who approves issuance and handles corrections or revocation?
  • Which key and status resources must remain available for verification?
