Credentials

Move through the parts of a Skill Passport. Each item keeps its source, and verification applies to that item—not automatically to everything in the record.

An achievement from its issuer

Credentials

Digital certificates and badges record achievements awarded by an institution. Their source remains visible as the learner brings them into a wider record.

Institution-issuedIssued by the awarding institution

Verification is specific to the credential and its issuer.

Capability with context

Skills

Skills help explain what learning represents. Each skill is shown with the achievement or evidence that supports it, whether institution-issued, learner-added or attested.

Source-linkedShown with its supporting achievement or context

Each supporting item carries its own source and verification status.

Work, shown in context

Projects

Research, designs, software, publications and other work let learners show how they applied what they learned. A project can include its own supporting evidence.

Learner-addedAdded and described by the learner

Learner-added work remains identified as learner-added unless separately attested.

Learning across settings

Experiences

Internships, volunteering, leadership and other meaningful activity can make a record more complete. The learner can describe the experience and add material that supports it.

Learner-addedSource and supporting material stay visible

An experience is not institution-verified simply because it appears in the passport.

A trusted confirmation

Achievements

A learner can record an achievement and request an educator or mentor to attest to it. The attestor reviews the submitted achievement and supporting evidence before confirming it.

AttestedConfirmed by an identified attestor

Attestation applies to the reviewed achievement and its evidence, not the entire passport.

The source behind the claim

Evidence

Documents, links and outputs can support credentials, skills, projects or experiences. The source stays attached to the item so a recipient can understand what is being presented.

Source retainedEvidence may be learner-added or issuer-provided

Evidence can support verification; it does not make every connected item verified automatically.

Source and verification are item-level. Learners can add to the record without changing an item's issuer or verification status.

05 · Capabilities connected to learning

Skills

Skills add meaning to what a credential represents. They can be connected to a programme, assessment or other supporting context, helping the learner explain capabilities alongside the achievement.

A skill's source and verification status remain tied to the evidence supporting that item.

06 · Work and what supports it

Projects & Evidence

Projects may include research, design, software, publications, community work or internship outcomes. Supporting links, documents and outputs add context while keeping the source of each item visible.

Learners can add project evidence. It is not institution-verified unless an institution separately issues or attests to it.

07 · Attested achievement

Attestations

Learners can add an achievement and supporting evidence, then ask an educator or mentor to review it. If approved, the attestation records who confirmed that achievement and when.
