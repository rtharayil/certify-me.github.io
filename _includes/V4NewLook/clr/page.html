What Is a Comprehensive Learner Record?

A Comprehensive Learner Record (CLR) brings a learner’s achievements together in a structured digital record. It connects the context around learning—from academic milestones to credentials and applied experience—while preserving the institution or organisation responsible for each achievement.

For institutions, the CLR provides a way to represent more of recognised learning without changing the authority of the underlying academic record.

Two records, distinct roles

Transcript vs CLR

The transcript remains the authoritative academic record. The CLR adds a broader view of learning and achievement around it.

Provenance & Verification

Connecting achievements should not erase where they came from. Issuer, evidence and available status information provide context for understanding a component; verification depends on the source and the checks supported for that credential.

Interoperable record formats

Standards

CLR 2.0 structures connected achievement records. Open Badges 3.0 represents individual badge achievements, and the W3C Verifiable Credentials model provides a foundation for verifiable claims. These formats support record exchange while each component retains its issuer and evidence context.

Institutional oversight

Institutional Governance

Institutions and professional bodies define which achievements belong in a record, who may issue them, and how evidence and access are governed. Academic authority stays with the institution; professional standards and credential status stay with their issuing body.
