University buyer journey: responsibilities and evidence

Begin with a governed programme and a shared acceptance checklist. The roles below help separate academic meaning, authoritative records, technical implementation and learner support.

Registrar: award and record governance

Agree issuer authority, award identifiers, dates, corrections and exceptions. Review digital diploma and qualification presentation, digital transcript presentation, verification procedures and the CLR's relationship to transcripts and learner records. A connected learner presentation does not automatically replace the authoritative transcript or SIS.

Provost and academic leadership: programme meaning

Define learning outcomes, assessment criteria and approving roles before issuance. Use reviewed outcome-to-skill relationships and microcredential programme scope without turning a taxonomy match into proof of competence.

Dean and faculty: achievement evidence

Choose evidence appropriate to the task, protect confidential feedback and distinguish participation from demonstrated competence. Review digital badge presentation alongside Open Badges achievement structures; a badge image is not the whole credential.

CIO and IT: supported interfaces and continuity

Test the digital credential lifecycle, current API scope and documented integration options. Use the W3C issuer–holder–verifier model to separate proof, issuer resources and receiver policy. Do not infer a native SIS connector or protocol from a general integration page.

IT/security: assurance, privacy and residency

Review the documented security and contractual data-residency scope. Agree access, retention, transfers, export and continuity in writing. Hosting availability is not a universal guarantee of region-only storage, backup and support access.

Career Services: learner presentation and opportunity research

Help learners explain assessed achievements through a connected learner record and the learner-facing Skill Passport experience on this page, while retaining issuer provenance. Relate evidence to occupations and separately sourced market context; do not treat engagement or a suggested role as a graduate-employment outcome.

Programme evaluation: measure the actual result

Use credential analytics with explicit event definitions. Compare an agreed pilot baseline with issuance, correction and engagement measures; assess academic and employment outcomes separately. The anonymous institutional cases provide scoped first-party examples, not universal ROI.
