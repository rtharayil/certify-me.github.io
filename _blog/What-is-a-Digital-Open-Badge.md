An Open Badge is a digital achievement credential that describes what was awarded, by whom and under which criteria. The badge image is its visual representation, not the whole record.

## Inspect achievement and evidence

The achievement description should identify participation, completion or assessment. Criteria explain what was required; evidence references help a receiving team understand what supports the award, subject to privacy and access rules.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/certificate-information.png" alt="Information of certificates" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Know which version is being discussed

The [Open Badges 3.0 guide](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards) explains the achievement model and its relationship to verifiable credentials. Older implementations and receivers may support different versions; test compatibility instead of assuming universal acceptance.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/open-badge-contents.png" alt=" Contents of open badge" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Do not confuse format with assessment quality

A valid proof may establish record integrity and signing authority, but not whether the issuer used an appropriate assessment. Use [institution-reviewed skills relationships](/skills-taxonomy-mapping) when interpreting capability statements.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/strong-profesional-presence.png" alt="Ways to build a strong professional presence" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Design a badge with an explicit purpose

For a fictional laboratory-safety milestone, describe the assessed task and the passing criterion rather than writing a broad claim of professional competence. Keep protected evidence in an authorized environment.

## Connect achievements over time

A [Comprehensive Learner Record](/comprehensive-learner-record) can connect badges with other achievements and preserve provenance. Decide how the institution will handle corrections, status changes and evidence access.

## Use documented scope

CertifyMe's [issuer certification article](/blog/certifyme-open-badge-3-0-certified) links the registry evidence. An issuer-role certification should not be expanded into a promise of every receiver's compatibility or every unrelated security capability.
