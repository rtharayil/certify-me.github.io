A verified credential should be accepted only for a defined purpose after issuer authority, supported proof, dates and applicable status are assessed. Verification is a decision process, not a universal trust label.

## Use a decision ladder

Read the claim; identify the issuer; check the securing mechanism; inspect dates/status; assess the criteria/evidence; and record whether the result is sufficient for the receiving organization's purpose. Each unresolved step should remain visible.

<img class="img-fluid r-16 d-block mx-auto" src="/img/blog/ultimate-guide-to-verified-credentials/w3c.jpg" alt="What Are Verified Credentials?">

## Keep roles distinct

The [W3C issuer–holder–verifier model](/blog/Understanding-W3C-Verifiable-Credentials.html) describes who issues, holds and checks a credential. The model does not independently establish accreditation, recipient identity or assessment quality.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/ultimate-guide-to-verified-credentials/Key-Components-Of-Verified-Credentials.png" alt="Key-Components-Of-Verified-Credentials">

## Treat format as one part of the decision

For [Open Badges achievement credentials](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards), inspect the issuer, achievement and criteria. A familiar badge graphic or a QR code is not proof by itself.

<img class="img-fluid r-16 d-block mx-auto" src="/img/blog/ultimate-guide-to-verified-credentials/IMG-20241127-WA0011.jpg" alt="image">

## Plan exceptions and continuity

Use the [institutional credential lifecycle](/platform-overview) to define corrections, status changes and issuer-resource continuity. If a proof or status resource is unavailable, record the uncertainty instead of treating it as a valid or revoked record.

<img class="img-fluid r-16 d-block mx-auto" src="/img/blog/ultimate-guide-to-verified-credentials/Credential Verification.png" alt="Credential Verification">

## Check connected records without losing accountability

A [Comprehensive Learner Record](/comprehensive-learner-record) may aggregate achievements, but each underlying claim retains an issuer and evidence boundary. A presentation layer should not conceal those dependencies.

<img class="img-fluid r-16 d-block mx-auto" src="/img/blog/ultimate-guide-to-verified-credentials/Verification image.png" alt="Verification image">

## Make verification decisions

An employer may accept a current professional-development achievement after proof and issuer checks, while still requiring a separate regulated license. The example shows a purpose-specific decision, not a claim that one credential replaces every qualification.
