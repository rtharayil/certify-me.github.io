A learner who voluntarily shares a credential may expose an institution's programme to a new audience. That is a possible marketing benefit, not a guaranteed endorsement, enrolment or hiring result. The learner's achievement should remain the primary purpose of the record.

## The marketing funnel is not automatic

A shared link may lead to a credential view, an onward programme visit, an enquiry and eventually an application. Each is a different event. One student posting a badge does not imply five likes, two clicks or one enrolment; no such conversion rate is established here.

| Stage | What it can indicate | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Award issued | Approved recognition delivered | Learning quality or public visibility |
| Optional share | Recipient chose to distribute a link | Endorsement or reach |
| Credential view | Record interaction under the measurement method | Unique human interest or competence |
| Programme visit | Onward site interaction | Enrolment or revenue |
| Enquiry/application | A recorded action | Causal effect of the credential programme |

Use [credential analytics scope](/credential-analytics.html) to define what is available and how events are counted. Record the cohort, period, bots/duplicates treatment and limits of attribution. Marketing ROI needs attributable outcomes and complete costs, not only page views.

## Make the destination trustworthy and useful

The credential page should identify the authorized issuer, achievement, criteria, dates and verification route. A crest is recognizable but can be copied; supported proof, issuer authority and status checks matter separately. Use [verification guidance](/certificate-verification) before calling every record “proof of skill.”

Institution-approved [white labeling](/blog/White-Labeling-Digital-Credentials.html) can create consistent presentation. It does not independently establish accreditation, authenticity, security or employer acceptance. Keep a programme call to action secondary to the award information.

## Three illustrative uses—not customer case studies

A leadership programme could describe a validated student role. A continuing-education course could recognize assessed completion. An alumni volunteering programme could record verified service under approved criteria. Each may offer optional sharing instructions. These examples assert no application increase, tripled reach or employment result.

For actual first-party implementation evidence, see [approved anonymous case studies](/case-studies.html). Their bounded outcomes are not a forecast for every programme, and customer anonymity should be retained.

## Recipient control and record continuity

Do not require a post, review or endorsement to earn the award. Obtain separate permission before republishing a learner's name, photo or story. Explain what a public link reveals and how corrections or restricted access are handled.

A credential does not “live forever” merely because it is online. Hosted pages, domains, proof and status resources have maintenance dependencies; exported records need compatible receivers. Define a continuity plan and avoid promising permanent circulation.

## Start with a measured campaign

Agree one approved award, an optional sharing message and a clear programme destination. Record actual interactions and report uncertainty rather than inferred viral effects.
