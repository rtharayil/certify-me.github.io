Digital badges support educational goals when the institution connects an assessed outcome to explicit criteria, evidence and an issued achievement—not when it simply awards an attractive image.

## Choose the achievement before the badge

Start with a learning outcome that can be observed. Specify the task, assessment method, minimum standard and approving team. Attendance and demonstrated competence are different achievements; the badge description should say which one was recognized.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/digital-badges-impact-on-learning-goals.png" alt="Impact of digital badges on learning goals" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Use an outcome–evidence–recognition checklist

For each badge, record: the outcome in the programme's language; the assessment artifact or protected evidence reference; the assessor's decision; the issuer; the award date; and any applicable expiry or status rules. For assessed capabilities, use [institution-reviewed skills mapping](/skills-taxonomy-mapping) rather than treating a badge label as proof of every related skill.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/badge-credibility-pyramid.png" alt="Credibility of badges" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Illustrative example: a research-methods milestone

A department could recognize a learner's completed research proposal after rubric-based assessment. The credential could identify the achievement and criteria while keeping confidential feedback in an authorized system. This example is a programme design pattern, not a claim about a named customer or a ready-made integration.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/embracing-digital-badges.png" alt="Embracing of digital badges through steps" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Use a standard where it fits the programme

An [Open Badges 3.0 achievement credential](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards) can describe issuer, achievement and evidence. Confirm the supported proof mechanism and receiving-system compatibility. A digital badge image alone does not establish conformance or interoperability.

## Keep the learner's record coherent

Related achievements can contribute to a [Comprehensive Learner Record with issuer provenance](/comprehensive-learner-record). Decide who may see evidence, how corrections are handled and whether the achievement remains meaningful after a programme changes.

## Evaluate educational usefulness, not just issuance volume

Review assessment completion, learner understanding and the burden on academic staff. Sharing and page views measure engagement, not skill attainment or employment. The [institutional credential lifecycle](/platform-overview) helps teams agree these controls before rollout. See [anonymous implementation evidence](/case-studies.html) for documented examples without assuming the same results.
