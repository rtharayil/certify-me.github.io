Digital achievements can make online learning milestones visible. Whether they improve engagement depends on the programme, assessment, feedback and student experience. A badge is not a substitute for an accessible course, useful feedback or a supportive teacher.

## Define the recognition students receive

An attendance badge records participation. A completion credential records the issuer's completion criteria. An assessed milestone describes a reviewed task and evidence. Do not label all three “proof of skill.” A verified social-media account is not an appropriate analogy for academic assessment or independent learner identity.

The [Open Badges 3.0 specification](https://www.imsglobal.org/spec/ob/v3p0/) describes achievement records. A supported proof check concerns integrity and signing authority; the institution remains responsible for the criteria and evidence. Use the [institutional OB3 guide](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards) for the deeper standards explanation.

## Build a small, purposeful pilot

1. Choose one course or co-curricular programme and one meaningful milestone. Avoid issuing a badge for every click.
2. Publish the criteria and show students what evidence is expected. Offer accessible alternatives and an appeal/correction route.
3. Have the academic or programme owner approve the award and the registrar/records team approve recipient association and retention.
4. Have IT test the actual LMS completion event, mapping, permissions, duplicate handling and failed delivery. Automation does not remove approval or support work.
5. Explain what is public and make sharing optional. Students should not have to advertise the institution to receive recognition.
6. Collect voluntary feedback and compare participation and assessed completion against a documented baseline.

**Illustrative example, not a university result:** a sustainability course recognizes a reviewed project proposal. The badge names that assessment, while peer discussion and attendance are measured separately. This example does not claim a retention increase, internship advantage or demonstrated customer success.

## Measure engagement and learning separately

Track eligible students, attempted tasks, approved completions, correction requests and accessibility issues. Record the cohort and period. Credential opens and shares measure interaction with the record, not mastery, belonging, enrolment retention or employment.

If the institution wants to study retention or learning effects, define a separate evaluation that considers other course changes and student differences. Do not infer causation from a rise in badge activity.

## Keep learner records and evidence governed

A [Comprehensive Learner Record](/comprehensive-learner-record) can connect approved academic and co-curricular achievements while retaining source provenance. It does not replace the institution's assessment or student-support systems. Public evidence references should not expose confidential work or feedback.

Use [credential infrastructure governance](/platform-overview) to assign approval, correction and maintenance owners. Export compatibility and issuer-key/status continuity still matter after students leave.
