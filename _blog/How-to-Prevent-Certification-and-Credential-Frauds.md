Preventing credential fraud requires checking the issuer, achievement, proof, dates and status together. A polished certificate, QR code or valid signature alone is not sufficient.

## Use an authority–integrity–status checklist

Authority: identify the issuer and confirm its right to award the achievement. Integrity: check the supported proof against the signed data. Status: inspect dates and applicable correction, expiry or revocation information. Record the decision and any exceptions.

## Treat a QR code as an access path

A QR code can take a reviewer to a record, but it is not itself proof. Check the destination and actual verification result using the [credential verification workflow](/certificate-verification); do not accept appearance or a familiar logo as authority.

## Understand what cryptography does not prove

The [W3C verification roles](/blog/Understanding-W3C-Verifiable-Credentials.html) distinguish proof checking from policy decisions. A signature does not independently establish a recipient's identity, accreditation or the quality of the assessment.

## Design controls before issuing

In the [institutional lifecycle](/platform-overview), separate award approval from issuance permissions. Define correction/status procedures, escalation for suspicious records and continuity for issuer resources. Ask which controls are actually included before promising automation.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/digital-credentialing-strategies.png" alt="Strategies of digital credentialing" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Check the credential format

For [Open Badges achievement credentials](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards), inspect issuer, achievement and criteria rather than inferring competence from the image. Compatibility with a receiving system should be tested.

## Illustrative exception handling

If a credential's proof validates but the awarding institution cannot be confirmed, do not mark the whole application verified. Escalate the authority question. If a status resource is unavailable, record that uncertainty rather than treating it as valid or revoked.

## Train reviewers on evidence, not slogans

Keep an agreed procedure and audit trail for manual decisions. No system can guarantee the absence of fraud; the purpose is to reduce avoidable risk and make unresolved questions explicit.
