Parchment comes up a lot when we talk to registrar's offices and higher-ed admin teams, and for good reason — it has been the industry standard for transcript exchange for a long time, and it does that specific job reliably.

But the conversation that usually follows is: *what happens after a credential is issued?* That question is increasingly the dividing line between platforms. Parchment was built to answer "how do we get official academic records from A to B securely?" CertifyMe was built to answer something broader: how do credentials stay useful, verifiable, and connected to a learner's career across their full lifecycle — through trust, records, skills, pathways, workforce relevance, and professional identity?

If your institution's need is transcript exchange, Parchment fits cleanly. If it's running a credentialing program that spans more than transcripts, this comparison explains where each platform starts to diverge.

## The Category Question: What Happens After Issuance?

A useful way to frame this comparison is not "which platform is better" but "what is each platform designed to do after a credential leaves the issuer?"

**Parchment** treats credential issuance as a delivery event — secure, reliable transfer of official academic records from institution to recipient to employer or next institution. The credential travels, the exchange is complete, and the record is on file. That model is exactly right for the use case it's built for.

**CertifyMe** treats issuance as the beginning of a credential's life, not the end of it. A credential issued on CertifyMe carries portable cryptographic proof; it can be part of a structured learning pathway that aggregates into a skills record; it can connect documented achievements to live job opportunities through Live Labour Market Intelligence; and it forms part of a professional identity that grows as the learner does.

## Credential Standards and Verification

### Parchment

Parchment has built its reputation on reliable, secure delivery of official academic records — transcripts, diplomas, degree verifications — between institutions and employers. Institutions trust it because it handles that workflow dependably and has done so at scale across higher education for many years.

**Where it's strong:**
- Secure academic transcript delivery and verification
- Trusted by institutions and employers for official record exchange
- Long-standing adoption across US higher education

**Worth knowing:** Parchment's focus is the academic record exchange workflow. If open interoperability standards are important, ask Parchment directly about its current W3C Verifiable Credentials and Open Badges 3.0 implementation, export options, and how issuer proof or status resources are maintained over time.

### CertifyMe

CertifyMe builds on W3C Verifiable Credentials, Open Badges 3.0 (independently 1EdTech certified — verifiable on the [1EdTech public registry](https://site.imsglobal.org/certifications)), and immutable credential technology. Its credentials carry tamper-evident cryptographic proof that can be checked outside CertifyMe’s presentation layer when issuer keys and relevant status resources remain available.

**Where it's strong:**
- Open, interoperable standards with independent certification
- Immutable, tamper-resistant verification
- Standards-based portability; confirm issuer-key and status-resource continuity for long-term programmes

For institutions whose credentialing strategy needs to work across regulatory environments, including emerging frameworks like the EU Digital Identity Wallet, the standards architecture is a meaningful differentiator.

## Academic Records vs. Broader Credential Infrastructure

This is the core of the comparison.

### Parchment

Parchment specializes in digital diplomas, academic transcripts, degree verification, transcript ordering, and student record management. For institutions whose credentialing need is precisely that — official academic record exchange — it is an excellent, purpose-built tool. There is no reason to look elsewhere for that specific workflow.

### CertifyMe

CertifyMe supports a much wider scope: digital certificates, open badges, professional certifications, continuing education credits, micro-credentials, learning pathways, and stackable credential programs. That breadth matters when an institution is running anything beyond official transcript exchange — training completions, professional certifications, workforce programs, continuing education, or multi-level certification tracks that need to aggregate into a learner's credential identity.

## Skills, Pathways, and Workforce Relevance

One of the clearest differences between the two platforms is what a credential means outside the moment of issuance.

**Parchment** issues academic records. Those records travel securely and are recognized by receiving institutions and employers. That is the intended scope, and it works.

**CertifyMe** is designed so credentials accumulate meaning over time. Structured learning pathways allow multi-stage certifications to build on each other. A [skills passport](/skills-passport) aggregates credentials into a growing skills record. [Live Labour Market Intelligence](/Job-Portal-Integration) connects that skills record to live job openings, salary benchmarks, and hiring signals across 20M+ live postings — so a learner's verified achievements connect directly to career action, not just recognition.

For universities and certification bodies trying to demonstrate graduate employment outcomes or workforce value, this is the gap between a record that confirms what happened and a credential program that actively supports what comes next.

## Learning Pathways

### Parchment

Parchment manages academic achievements after they are complete — it is a record system, not a progression architecture. Support for multi-stage learning journeys, stackable credentials, or milestone-based certification tracks is not part of its design, because that is not the problem it is solving.

### CertifyMe

Structured learning pathways, progressive certifications, milestone tracking, and stackable credentials are built into the CertifyMe platform. For universities running certificate programs alongside degrees, or certification bodies with multi-tier tracks, this enables the kind of credential architecture that is outside Parchment's record-delivery scope. ## When Parchment Is the Better Fit

Parchment is likely the right choice when:

- **Academic transcript exchange is the primary use case.** If your institution's core need is the secure, reliable transfer of official transcripts between students, institutions, and employers, Parchment has a long and reliable track record doing exactly this.
- **You are a registrar or enrollment management team** focused on diploma issuance, transcript ordering, and student record management rather than running a broader credentialing program.
- **Your credential types are limited to diplomas and academic records.** Parchment is purpose-built for this scope and does not try to be something else.

<br>

## When CertifyMe Is the Better Fit

CertifyMe is likely the right choice when:

- **You are running a credentialing program beyond transcripts** — certificates, badges, professional certifications, continuing education, micro-credentials, or multi-stage certification tracks.
- **Open standards matter for your strategy.** If credentials need portable proof, cross-platform use, or alignment with emerging frameworks like the EU Digital Identity Wallet, CertifyMe's 1EdTech-certified OB3 and W3C VC architecture is the more appropriate foundation. Confirm issuer-key and status-resource continuity as part of procurement.
- **You need structured learning pathways.** Multi-stage certification tracks, stackable credentials, and milestone-based programs require a progression architecture that is outside Parchment's record-delivery scope.
- **Workforce and career outcomes are part of the brief.** Live Labour Market Intelligence connecting credentials to live job openings is outside Parchment's stated scope as an academic record exchange platform.
- **You serve multiple program types under one roof** — a university that runs both continuing education and professional certifications alongside degree programs, or a certification body with tiered credential tracks, needs the broader infrastructure CertifyMe is built for.
- **Extensive white-labeling and branded learner experience** are requirements beyond the document itself.

More context on what a full credential infrastructure platform covers is at the [higher education credential platform overview](/credentials-higher-education) and the [four-platform comparison covering Accredible, Certifier, Credly, and CertifyMe](/blog/digital-credentialing-platforms-2026-comparison).

## The Bottom Line

Parchment and CertifyMe are not competing for the same use case, which makes this comparison more straightforward than most. Parchment is a strong, trusted tool for academic transcript and diploma exchange — it is built for that job and does it well.

CertifyMe is built for the broader question of what credential infrastructure needs to do: establish trust through open standards, accumulate into learner records, connect to skills data and workforce intelligence, and support a learner's professional identity over time. If your credentialing scope extends beyond the official academic transcript — certificates, badges, professional certifications, learning pathways, or anything that needs to stay useful and verifiable across a career — that is the problem CertifyMe is designed to solve.

The deciding question is usually: are you managing official academic records, or are you running a credentialing program? The answer tends to make the choice clear.
