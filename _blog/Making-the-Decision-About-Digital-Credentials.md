Choosing digital credentials is an institutional governance decision before it is a design-tool decision. Define the achievement, trust model, record requirements and operating responsibilities before choosing a platform.

## Use four procurement questions

What is being awarded? Who approves it? How will a receiving team verify it? Who maintains it after issuance? These questions distinguish certificate presentation from the [full credential lifecycle](/platform-overview).

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/digital-credential-pillars.png" alt="Four pillars of digital credentials " style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Separate format from verification

Determine which versions and securing mechanisms a programme needs. The [W3C credential model](/blog/Understanding-W3C-Verifiable-Credentials.html) and [Open Badges 3.0](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards) explain different but related concepts. A standards claim is not a guarantee of every receiving-system integration.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/mistakes-to-avoid.png" alt="Challenges of digital transformation" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Define records and evidence

Confirm export, provenance, corrections, dates and status resources. If the programme connects multiple achievements, assess a [Comprehensive Learner Record](/comprehensive-learner-record) without assuming that it replaces the transcript or SIS.

<img class="img-fluid r-16" src="/img/blog/digital-credential-myth.png" alt="Myths of digital credentials" style="display: block; margin: 0 auto;">

## Define the buyer responsibilities

Registrars govern records; academic teams approve assessed outcomes; IT reviews access, security and supported interfaces; career services determine how evidence and [workforce information](/workforce-intelligence) should support learners.

## Pilot against explicit acceptance criteria

Choose one real programme and test approval, issuance, recipient access, verification, export and an exception. Record missing capabilities as open requirements. Avoid a rollout decision based only on a template gallery or a demonstration that has not exercised failure paths.

## Use a requirements-led comparison

The [comparison hub](/compare/) is a starting point, not a substitute for current provider documentation and contract terms. Confirm features, pricing, authentication, residency and continuity in writing. Do not infer a competitor lacks a feature because one public page omits it.

## Plan your next step

Discuss the actual award, evidence and implementation requirements with your institution. [Request an institutional demo](https://info.certifyme.online/request-demo).
