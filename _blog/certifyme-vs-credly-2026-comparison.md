If you've been evaluating credentialing platforms lately, Credly has likely come up early in the conversation. Pearson's backing gives it real weight in corporate learning circles, and for enterprise workforce development inside that ecosystem it has earned its reputation. But brand recognition is the wrong starting point for the comparison — the more useful question is: **what happens after a credential is issued?**

Both platforms will get a digital credential into a learner's hands. Where they diverge is in the infrastructure built around everything that follows: how the credential stays verifiable over time, how it connects to skills records, whether it supports multi-stage programs, how portable it is outside a single enterprise ecosystem, and what workforce relevance it carries for the individual who holds it.

CertifyMe is built as broader credential infrastructure across all of those dimensions — trust, verifiable records, skills, pathways, workforce relevance, and identity portability. Credly is designed primarily for enterprise internal skills recognition, with strong integration into Pearson's broader learning ecosystem. These reflect different scope decisions, not just quality differences. See the [digital credential maturity model](/digital-credential-maturity/) for a framework that maps where different platform capabilities sit across the credential lifecycle.

## Two Platforms, Different Starting Points

Both companies sell "digital credentials," but they didn't start from the same place, and it shows in what they prioritize.

Credly grew up inside corporate L&D. It's built around recognizing what employees know — skills, competencies, internal certifications — and making that visible across a workforce. If you're already in Pearson's orbit and your core use case is workforce badge programs, Credly has a long track record doing exactly that.

CertifyMe came at the problem from a different angle: how does a credential remain useful across its full life — across employers, platforms, time horizons, and regulatory contexts? That led toward open standards, immutable records, structured skills metadata, multi-stage learning pathways, and workforce relevance tools built for the individual credential holder, not just the enterprise issuing the credential.

<br>

## Credential Standards and Verification

This is where the platforms diverge most meaningfully for buyers with portability, regulatory, or long-term verifiability requirements.

### Credly

Credly's verification is solid and enterprise-grade — IT teams in large organizations have deployed it at scale. Badges are shareable across LinkedIn and professional networks, and that established reliability is a significant part of its value in enterprise environments.

**Where it's strong:**
- Reliable, enterprise-grade badge verification with long operational history
- Strong professional network sharing, including LinkedIn
- Trusted by large enterprise IT and HR teams

**Worth verifying with the vendor:** W3C Verifiable Credentials is not Credly's primary publicly documented focus, and independent 1EdTech OB3 certification is not clearly documented in their public materials. For credentials that need to work across platforms, within regulatory frameworks, or beyond the current vendor relationship, confirm the current standards roadmap directly with Credly before committing.

### CertifyMe

CertifyMe builds on W3C Verifiable Credentials, Open Badges 3.0, and immutable credential technology. Its standards-based proofs can be validated outside CertifyMe's presentation layer when issuer keys and relevant status resources remain available, and cryptographic design makes post-issuance alteration substantially harder.

**Where it's strong:**
- Independent 1EdTech OB3 certification, publicly verifiable on the [1EdTech registry](https://site.imsglobal.org/certifications)
- W3C Verifiable Credentials as the underlying data model
- Immutable, tamper-resistant credential records
- Long-term portability across platforms and regulatory contexts

For credentials that need to function within frameworks being built on the W3C VC standard — EU Digital Identity Wallet, national digital identity schemes — CertifyMe's architecture is the more clearly documented choice. See [credential verification in practice](/certificate-verification) and the [CertifyMe OB3 certification explainer](/blog/certifyme-open-badge-3-0-certified).

<br>

## Learning Journeys vs. Skill Snapshots

A single badge documents one achievement at a point in time. A structured credential pathway documents where someone started and how far they've progressed — and that distinction matters depending on who you serve.

### Credly

Credly is well-suited to recognizing individual skills and achievements as they happen within a workforce. Straightforward enterprise badge programs that don't require multi-stage architecture are its home territory, and for that use case it performs well.

### CertifyMe

CertifyMe supports structured credential progression: stackable credentials, milestone tracking, and multi-level certification tracks. For universities, professional certification bodies, or organizations running programs that build on each other across time — rather than issuing one-off skill badges — this infrastructure is a functional requirement, not a feature preference.

CertifyMe also attaches structured skills metadata to each credential, supporting Comprehensive Learner Record-style accumulation of achievement data over time. Universities and multi-tier certification providers find significantly more utility here than a single-company L&D team typically would.

<br>

## What Happens After the Credential: Workforce Relevance

Here's where CertifyMe covers ground that Credly's current design does not focus on: connecting the credential to live workforce signals for the individual holder.

Credly's approach to career outcomes is built around workforce visibility — making skills visible to employers within its network and providing HR analytics for workforce planning. That signal flows primarily inward (to the organization's HR function) rather than outward (to the credential holder navigating the job market).

CertifyMe adds **Live Labour Market Intelligence** to every issued credential — real, current data on which roles, salaries, and hiring signals align with the skills the learner just earned, drawn from 20M+ live postings across 25,000+ companies. It turns the credential into an actionable career signal for the holder, not just a record visible to employers in a network. For higher-ed institutions and workforce programs trying to demonstrate real employment outcomes rather than completion counts, this is a meaningful category distinction.

## Branding and White-Labeling

### Credly

Branded experiences are available on Credly, but from publicly documented information, deeper customization tends to run through enterprise service agreements rather than being available self-serve. Verify the current white-label options directly with Credly for your specific requirements.

### CertifyMe

CertifyMe is built white-label from the ground up — custom domains, branded portals, organization-specific fonts, branded email templates, and credential landing pages that reflect the issuing institution rather than a third-party vendor. For organizations that need the entire credential experience — from issuance through verification — to carry their brand consistently, this is a meaningful architectural difference.

Credly has years of experience in workforce planning and talent management at scale, particularly for organizations already operating within Pearson's broader learning ecosystem. If you're deeply integrated with Pearson tools, Credly's enterprise connections are a genuine advantage.

CertifyMe covers the enterprise fundamentals — bulk issuance, API integrations, multi-organization support, workflow automation — with flexibility built around an open-standards architecture. The same platform serves a university, a professional association, and a corporate training program without forcing them into a workforce-first operational model. CertifyMe's analytics cover issuance, verification, learning pathway completion, and credential sharing alongside program performance — a broader picture that serves both credential-management and learner-side outcomes measurement. ## Standards Compliance and Certification: The Detail That Matters

For organizations whose credentials need to remain verifiable across systems and over time, the standards compliance question deserves more scrutiny than a feature matrix provides.

CertifyMe holds **independent 1EdTech certification for Open Badges 3.0 conformance**, publicly verifiable on the [1EdTech certification registry](https://site.imsglobal.org/certifications). This is a materially different claim from self-reported OB3 support — it means an independent body has tested CertifyMe's OB3 implementation against the specification. CertifyMe also supports W3C Verifiable Credentials as the underlying data model, enabling cryptographic proof outside a single presentation interface when issuer keys and relevant status resources remain available. See the [CertifyMe OB3 certification explainer](/blog/certifyme-open-badge-3-0-certified) for full context.

Whether Credly holds equivalent independent OB3 certification is not clearly documented in their public materials — verify with the vendor. Credly supports Open Badges and is a well-established enterprise platform. For internal skills recognition at enterprise scale within Pearson's ecosystem, it is a capable and proven choice. For credentials that need to work across platforms, regulatory contexts, or time horizons beyond the vendor relationship, open-standards certification provides a different level of documented assurance.

For enterprise buyers weighing the standards question within a full evaluation framework, the guide on [choosing a digital credential management platform](/blog/What-is-Digital-Credential-Management-Platform) covers the procurement questions that reveal a platform's actual enterprise readiness.

## When Credly is a Better Fit

- Your primary use case is enterprise internal workforce skills recognition — making employee competencies visible within an organization's talent management systems
- You are already operating within Pearson's learning ecosystem and benefit from deep integration with their tooling
- Your credentials are primarily for internal visibility and professional network sharing rather than cross-platform or regulatory portability
- Enterprise L&D analytics focused on workforce planning and talent mobility are the metrics that matter most to your stakeholders
- You are a large enterprise with dedicated resources to manage the deployment and vendor relationship within a known ecosystem

<br>

## When CertifyMe is a Better Fit

- Your credentials need to remain portable and verifiable independent of any single vendor — across platforms, regulatory contexts, or over longer time horizons
- You need independently certified Open Badges 3.0 conformance (publicly verifiable on the 1EdTech registry) and/or W3C Verifiable Credentials support for regulatory or interoperability requirements
- You serve universities, professional certification bodies, or multi-stage training programs where stackable credentials and milestone tracking are core requirements
- You need extensive white-labeling that reflects your institution's brand self-serve across the entire credential experience, from issuance through verification
- Credentials need to carry live workforce relevance data for individual holders — not just internal skills visibility for HR
- Comprehensive Learner Record or structured skills-tagging capabilities are part of your programme requirements
- You need one platform to serve multiple distinct organizational types — higher education, certification bodies, enterprise training — on a flexible, open-standards foundation

<br>

## The Bottom Line

Credly and CertifyMe are both legitimate platforms, but they are solving different problems. Credly is a strong choice if your world is enterprise skills recognition within Pearson's ecosystem, and you value the depth of integration that relationship provides.

CertifyMe is built for organizations that see a credential as more than a workforce badge — as infrastructure that needs to hold up over time, work outside a single ecosystem, reflect the issuing organization's brand consistently, and actively help the holder understand its value in the job market via Live Labour Market Intelligence.
