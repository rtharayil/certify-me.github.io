## How university credential verification works

1. Locate the issued record through its link, QR code or supported credential file.
2. Inspect the issuer, recipient reference, achievement, criteria and evidence.
3. Validate supported cryptographic proof against the appropriate issuer information.
4. Check applicable expiry, status and revocation resources.
5. Record what was checked and resolve unavailable resources or inconsistent information.

## Open standards and implementation

[Open Badges 3.0](/blog/why-institutions-should-embrace-open-badges-3-0-standards) uses the [W3C Verifiable Credentials model](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-1.1/) for structured achievement claims. Agree supported credential versions, proof mechanisms, key rotation, status services and verification policies. Standards do not eliminate dependencies on maintained issuer resources.

## Institutional use cases

Registrars can define verification policies for digital diplomas and certificates. Receiving universities can review qualification evidence. Employers can inspect shared achievement records without treating a signed credential as independent identity verification.

Connect verified achievements to [skills taxonomy mapping](/skills-taxonomy-mapping) and [Comprehensive Learner Records](/comprehensive-learner-record), while preserving issuer provenance.
